What is Bilty (Lorry Receipt)? Complete Guide for Indian Transporters 2026

By Cargo Simplify Team Published: April 10, 2026 Last updated: April 10, 2026 ~8 min read

If you work in Indian transport or logistics, you've heard the word bilty hundreds of times. But what exactly is a bilty, what must it contain, and why does every lorry movement require one? This guide answers all of it.

What is a Bilty?

A bilty is a document issued by a transporter (or lorry broker) when they take charge of goods from a shipper for delivery to a consignee. It is the primary proof that the transporter has accepted the goods and is legally responsible for delivering them safely to the destination.

Think of a bilty as the receipt + contract in a single document. It says: "I, the transporter, have received these goods from you and promise to deliver them to [consignee] at [destination] for the agreed freight amount."

In simple terms: A bilty is the "receipt" you get from a transporter when you hand over your goods for delivery.

Bilty vs LR vs Consignment Note — What's the Difference?

These terms are used interchangeably across India, but they all refer to the same document:

TermFull FormWhere Used
Bilty—North India, Gujarat, Rajasthan (common in Hindi-speaking regions)
LRLorry ReceiptSouth India, formal/accounting contexts
Consignment Note—Legal/formal documentation
GRGoods ReceiptSome regions in India
PWBParcel Way BillRailways and courier context

For GST purposes, the document is called a Consignment Note — but in the field, lorry brokers and drivers still call it a bilty.

What a Bilty Must Contain

A standard Indian bilty / LR should include the following fields:

Consignor Details

  • Full name of the shipper
  • Address and city
  • GSTIN (if GST-registered)
  • Contact number

Consignee Details

  • Full name of the recipient
  • Delivery address
  • GSTIN (if GST-registered)
  • Contact number

Goods Details

  • Description of goods
  • Number of packages / units
  • Gross weight (in kg)
  • Declared value (for insurance)

Transport Details

  • Lorry / truck number
  • Driver name
  • From city → To city
  • Date of despatch
  • Freight amount
  • Payment terms (To-Pay / Paid / To-be-billed)

Additionally, the bilty must carry the LR number (sequential number), the transporter's name and GSTIN, and the transporter's signature or stamp.

Types of Bilty in India

1. To-Pay Bilty (Freight To-Pay)

The freight amount is paid by the consignee (recipient) at the destination when they receive the goods. This is the most common type for B2B cargo movement.

2. Paid Bilty (Freight Paid)

The consignor (shipper) pays the freight upfront when handing over the goods. Common when the seller wants to absorb delivery costs.

3. To-Be-Billed (TBB)

Freight is settled via invoice at month-end. Common for businesses with long-term transporter contracts where trips are aggregated and settled monthly.

4. GST-Compliant Bilty

Since GST introduction, bilties issued by GTA (Goods Transport Agencies) must comply with specific GST documentation requirements. The transporter's GSTIN, SAC code for transport services, and GST amount (if applicable) must appear.

Legal Importance of a Bilty

A bilty is a legally binding document under the Carriage by Road Act, 2007. Once the transporter signs the bilty:

  • They become responsible for safe delivery of the goods
  • Any damage, loss, or shortage must be compensated by the transporter
  • The bilty is evidence in court if goods are lost or damaged
  • The consignee can claim delivery rights using the bilty copy

This is why a lost bilty copy can trigger serious legal and payment disputes between shippers, brokers, and fleet owners.

Bilty and GST / E-Way Bill

Under GST, the bilty is linked to two compliance requirements:

1. Goods Transport Agency (GTA) Invoice

If the transporter is registered as a GTA under GST, they must issue a GST invoice for the freight charges. This invoice references the bilty number.

2. E-Way Bill

For goods valued above ₹50,000 moving between states (and in most intra-state scenarios), an e-way bill must accompany the bilty. The e-way bill number is typically written on or attached to the bilty. Missing an e-way bill attracts a penalty of ₹10,000 or the tax amount — whichever is higher.
